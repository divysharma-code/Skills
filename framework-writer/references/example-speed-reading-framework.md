# Worked example: Speed Reading (skill/habit archetype)

This is a real output of the framework-writer shape, kept as a calibration example.
It shows how the six-part template renders for a **skill/habit** topic: layers become
drills, the decision table becomes a mode-selection table, and the adoption plan
becomes a practice schedule.

---

## Why You Read Slow (the 3 culprits)

1. **Subvocalization** — you "hear" every word in your head as you read. This caps you near talking speed (~150-250 wpm).
2. **Regression** — your eyes involuntarily jump back to re-read words you already saw, out of anxiety not need.
3. **Narrow fixation width** — your eyes stop on almost every single word instead of grabbing 3-4 words per stop.

Speed reading isn't a trick — it's just training yourself out of these three habits.

## The Framework: 5 Layers, Build in Order

### Layer 1 — Eye Mechanics
- **Pacing**: Use a finger, pen, or cursor to guide your eyes across the line faster than feels comfortable. This is the single highest-leverage habit — it forces pace and cuts regression because your eyes trust the pointer instead of double-checking themselves.
- **Chunking**: Instead of reading word-by-word, train your eyes to land 3 times per line (not 8-10). Each landing spot should absorb a phrase, not a word.
- **Reduce regression**: Use an index card or your hand to cover text you've already read, physically blocking the backward eye-jump.

### Layer 2 — Killing Subvocalization
- Hum, count ("1-2-3-4") under your breath, or chew gum while reading — occupying your "inner voice" forces your brain to process text visually instead of phonetically.
- This feels wrong at first (comprehension dips) — that's expected and temporary as your visual-processing pathway takes over.

### Layer 3 — Purpose-Driven Reading
Not all text deserves the same speed. Before reading anything, pick a mode:

| Mode | Speed | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Skimming** | Very fast | Get the gist, decide if worth reading fully |
| **Scanning** | Very fast | Hunting for a specific fact/keyword |
| **Speed reading** | 2-3x normal | General content, need full comprehension |
| **Study reading** | Normal/slow | Dense, technical, must-retain material |

Rule of thumb: preview before you dive. Spend 60 seconds skimming headings, bold text,
first/last sentences of paragraphs before committing to full-speed reading.

### Layer 4 — Practice Drills
1. **Timed sprints**: Read a page, note time, calculate WPM. Track weekly.
2. **Metronome drilling**: Use a tool that forces a scroll pace slightly faster than comfortable.
3. **Peripheral vision drills**: Practice seeing the first and last word of a line without moving your eyes directly to them.
4. **Comprehension checks**: Every drill ends with 3-5 questions about what you read — speed without comprehension is skimming with extra steps.

### Layer 5 — Comprehension Safety Net
- **80% rule**: If comprehension drops below ~80% at a given speed, dial back until it recovers, then push again gradually.
- **Match speed to material difficulty**: Push hard on fiction/narrative. Slow down on technical/legal/unfamiliar vocabulary — expected, not failure.
- **Re-read strategically, not compulsively**: Finish the paragraph first; most "regressions" resolve from context alone.

## A 4-Week Starter Plan
- **Week 1**: Add the pointer/pacer only. Measure baseline WPM.
- **Week 2**: Add anti-subvocalization during pacer drills, 15 min/day.
- **Week 3**: Add chunking drills — land 3x per line instead of word-by-word.
- **Week 4**: Combine all three + the preview habit. Re-measure WPM and comprehension.

## Realistic Targets
Most beginners go from ~200 wpm to 400-500 wpm within a month with daily practice,
while holding comprehension steady. 1000+ wpm claims usually trade away comprehension
and are mostly skimming rebranded — see the myth below.

## Myths to Avoid
- ❌ "Speed reading lets you read anything at 1000+ wpm with full retention" — physiologically implausible for dense/new material.
- ❌ "Subvocalization is always bad" — it helps for poetry, legal text, anything where precision matters.
- ❌ "Faster is always better" — the goal is matching speed to purpose, not maximizing WPM.
