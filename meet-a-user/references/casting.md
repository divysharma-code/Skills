# Casting: turning a page into one concrete person

A user type is a population (the Auth Submitter page describes on the order of 80,000 people across four archetypes). A synthetic user has to feel like **one** of them. Casting collapses the population into a single, consistent individual, using only what the page supports — and does it **differently every time**, so a teammate never meets the same synth twice.

## Pull these from the page (or the evidence card)

Read the live page — or, once built, the evidence card (`evidence-card.md`) — and extract:

- **Who they are / also known as** — the role label and its synonyms.
- **Credentials, specialties, settings** — the ranges the person can plausibly come from.
- **Primary products and tools** — what they actually operate.
- **Goals, jobs, pains, behaviors** — the substance of what they will say.
- **Verbatims** — direct quotes; the richest voice cues.
- **Archetype / segment descriptions** — how one slice behaves differently from another.
- **Stakeholder web** — who they depend on and hand off to.

## Instantiate one person

Choose a concrete instance **only from the ranges the page names**. Never add a specialty, plan, EHR, or tool the page does not mention.

- **Name & avatar:** give a first name and a person-emoji avatar so the person feels real, and state once that both are labels, not evidence. Match the avatar's gender to the page where it states one; otherwise it is cosmetic and implies nothing about the real population. ("Marisa, and the name's just a label, not from the research.")
- **Specialty / setting / plan / tools:** pick a plausible combination from the page's lists.
- **Tenure and fluency:** set by the diversity axes below.
- **Voice:** derive from verbatims and behaviors. An expert sounds fast, clipped, a little impatient, proud of clean work. A newer user sounds tentative, asks what things mean, leans on support. Match the register the quotes show.

Show a short **cast card** before the conversation as three tight lines, not a dense block: **Who** (name as a label, role and specialty, plan, tools), **Axes** (the diversity axes this person sits on), and **Lens** (internal vs. external, customer, and any confidence caveat). Invite the teammate to adjust or `recast`.

## Diversity: how to make each cast a different person

Diversity must come from **what the evidence already varies on**, never from invented personality. Walk this ladder per user type and pick the first tier that applies; most types land on tier 3.

**Tier 1 — Archetype matrix.** If the page defines behavioral archetypes (Auth Submitter's Agency × Mastery 2×2), use them. Each is its own gated slice and genuinely changes answers. Rare — most types have none.

**Tier 2 — Documented behavioral segments.** If the page names segments without a full matrix (Auth Submitter's portal-adopter vs. fax/phone-reliant; many pages carry a "segments to keep distinct" note), cast on those.

**Tier 3 — The universal grounded axes (the common case).** When there is neither a matrix nor named segments, vary the person on:

- **Tenure: new ↔ seasoned.** Grounded wherever the page mentions experience, ramp, a learning curve, or novice-vs-expert behavior — nearly always. A newcomer and a veteran in the same role are visibly different people and answer the same question differently.
- **Which documented pain dominates.** Same evidence, different *hot button*: one instance leads with the thing that wastes their time, another with the thing that blocks their outcome. This is what makes two same-role people feel distinct without adding a single unsupported fact.
- **A fresh draw of the named ranges (the casting dice).** Rotate specialty × health plan × EHR/tool across instances. A seasoned cardiology person on Humana in Epic and a newer ortho one on Geisinger in Athena are clearly different, both fully grounded.

**Conditional axis — Customer / health-plan lens.** Offer "meet this user as they are at Customer X" **only when the page carries substantial per-customer research** — a customer with its own verbatims, metrics, and distinct documented behaviors, not merely a client listed in Scope. This is evidence-gated exactly like the confidence gate: if the depth isn't there, the axis stays dark (and can be named as a gap). Today most pages skew to one plan and won't qualify; the axis is built to light up when the research catches up, never to invite fabrication now.

Axes are chosen from **available** evidence, never forced. For a single-seniority role (e.g., a VP) tenure variance may be thin — there, lean on dominant-pain plus the range draw instead.

## Anti-repetition

- At cast time, state the axes and where this person sits — e.g., "seasoned · leads with the multi-portal burden · GI · Geisinger · Epic."
- On `recast` or a new session, deliberately move to a **different** point on those axes, and say what changed ("last time you met a veteran leading with the auth-number lag; here's a newer submitter fighting missing-info pends").
- Keep the instance **stable within a session** — do not silently change their plan or EHR mid-conversation.

## Let the archetype/axis drive behavior

Axes are not flavor; they change answers. The same question ("do you attach documentation up front?") should get a different honest answer from a seasoned Empowered Expert than from a Loyal Learner. For Auth Submitter's four quadrants:

- **Empowered Expert** (high/high): knows requirements cold, can get an addendum, accepts a nudge when the evidence is compelling, educates the provider.
- **Autonomous Apprentice** (high agency, low mastery): close to the provider but green; accepts nudges often to earn an auto-approval; unsure which requirements apply.
- **Proficient Passenger** (low agency, high mastery): fluent but order-bound, cannot get missing documents, rarely accepts nudges; nearest the third-party subtype.
- **Loyal Learner** (low/low): new and constrained, does not trust nudges, does not know where to find documentation.

That difference is the value.

## Recasting commands

- `recast` — new instance of the same type/archetype, moved to a different axis point.
- `switch archetype` / `switch variant` — re-run the gate and instantiate a different quadrant or subtype. Announce the change.
- If a mode's artifact clearly targets a different specialty or archetype than the one cast (a therapy ticket while cast as cardiology), say so and offer to recast, the way an honest interviewee flags "that's not my area."

## Casting a panel

To convene several voices at once (Panel mode), cast **one concrete person per participant**, each drawn from its own page's evidence and each passing the gate on its own. Give every panelist a **distinct avatar** so they stay separable, keep each turn to a line or two, and let the contrast do the work: pick participants whose documented stances actually differ (the VP who avoids a tool, the specialist who lives in it). For 3+ people, distill each evidence card via a subagent so the main context stays lean. Do not blur one page's evidence into another's; if a panelist would only be guessing on the shared question (it is not their seat), have them say so rather than fill the silence. Close with a cross-seat synthesis, not a per-person recap.
